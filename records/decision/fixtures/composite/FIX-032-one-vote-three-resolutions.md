# FIX-032 — one vote, three resolutions; and a vote of its own

> **Fictional worked fixture.** Ostley Harbour Board, its people and its numbers are invented.

**Proves** that the unit of a candidate is the authoritative act: one motion carried on one vote is
one composite candidate with each resolution traceable, and a separate vote is a separate candidate.

## Sources

**A — Board minute, `board/2025-03-19.md`**, *Ostley Harbour Board, 19 March 2025*

> Item 6 — Marina redevelopment.
>
> **Resolved:**
> 1. That the marina redevelopment be approved in principle.
> 2. That the Harbour Engineer be authorised to procure design services within the approved budget.
> 3. That no works contract be let until the board has considered a final business case.
>
> Moved by the Chair. Carried, 6–2.
>
> Item 7 — Berthing fees. The board then voted separately on the 2025/26 berthing fees schedule, as
> tabled. Carried, 5–3.

## Expected

- **Two candidates**, both dated `2025-03-19`.
- **Item 6 is one composite candidate.** One motion, one vote. Its three resolutions are each their own
  span and their own `consequence` contribution — approval in principle; the Harbour Engineer's
  authority to procure design; no works contract before a final business case — so that a later act
  can supersede one without the others.
- **Item 7 is its own candidate.** A separate vote is a separate act, whatever it shares with item 6.
- Both owners are **missing**: the Chair moved item 6, and a mover is not an owner.

## Must not

- **Split item 6 into three candidates.** Each resolution would look separately authorised, and each
  would carry the whole vote as though it had been taken on it alone.
- **Fold item 6's resolutions into one undivided statement**, losing which resolution a later act
  changes.
- **Merge item 7 into item 6** because both were decided at one meeting, under one heading or on one
  day.

## What this fixture detects

Both ways of misplacing the act: carving one vote into several apparent decisions, and gathering
several votes into one.
