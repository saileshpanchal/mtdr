# FIX-014 — three wordings, one commitment

> **Fictional worked fixture.** Meridian Mutual is invented. The paraphrase-set design follows the
> value-language research's own convergence test: humans immediately recognise the same commitment;
> interpretation has to prove it.

**Proves:** the same commitment expressed three ways across three sources converges on **one**
candidate — on admissible identity evidence, not on wording similarity — while a fourth,
similar-sounding statement stays out.

## Sources

**A — Proposition paper, `mortgage-journey-proposition.md`, dated 2026-02-10**
> Under the digital-mortgage decision, we commit to reducing abandoned mortgage applications by
> improving the digital application journey. Owner: Retail Director Hana Suzuki.

**B — Steering pack, `retail-steering-2026-03.md`**
> The mortgage workstream's goal, per the February proposition: improve customer completion through
> a simplified application journey.

**C — Town hall notes, `q1-townhall.md`**
> Hana's team is focused on increasing successful applications by removing unnecessary customer
> friction in mortgages.

**D — Savings paper, `savings-onboarding.md`, dated 2026-03-20**
> We will reduce abandonment in savings onboarding by streamlining identity checks. Owner:
> Digital Director Priya Nair.

## Expected

- A, B and C → **one CandidateAssembly**. Grouping basis: **explicit cross-reference** (B cites the
  February proposition) and **ownership** (Hana Suzuki / "Hana's team" for the same subject) —
  named evidence, not "similar wording". The three wordings map to the same `commitment`.
- D → **a separate candidate** (or contributions held apart): different journey, different owner,
  different decision lineage — despite near-identical abandonment/streamlining language.
- Completeness on the mortgage candidate: `commitment`, `beneficiary` (applicants, evidenced via
  the journey), `committed-by`, `linked-decision` supported; `baseline`, `measure`,
  `falsifying-signal`, `time` missing.

## Must not

- Produce three mortgage candidates because there are three wordings.
- Merge D on lexical similarity — the wording is the trap; the identity evidence separates it.
- Cite "semantic similarity" as the grouping basis for A/B/C when explicit cross-reference and
  ownership are available. Right result by the weak basis is a failure.
- Invent a completion-rate baseline to make the candidate look measurable.
