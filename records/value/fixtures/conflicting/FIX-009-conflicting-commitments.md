# FIX-009 — conflicting commitments: the tension is the finding

> **Fictional worked fixture.** Ashworth Building Society is invented.

**Proves:** two valid commitments in genuine tension are both real, the tension is recorded on each,
and no skill resolves it — resolution requires organisational authority that no interpretation
carries.

## Sources

**A — Cost programme charter, `cts-programme-charter.md`, dated 2026-03-10**

> Under the FY26 efficiency mandate we commit to reducing contact-centre cost-to-serve from the FY25
> baseline of £6.2m to £5.4m by FY27 year end. Owner: Operations Director Marcus Boyd. Recognised in
> the contact-centre cost line.

**B — Service commitment, `service-standards-2026.md`, dated 2026-03-24**

> Under the Board's service mandate we commit to answering 90% of vulnerable-customer calls within
> three minutes by Q4 2026, from the current 71% (February service MI). Owner: Customer Director
> Amara Okonkwo. Tracked through the published service scorecard.

**C — Planning note, `h2-planning-note.md`, dated 2026-06-02**

> Both targets currently assume the same contact-centre staffing envelope; the resourcing review has
> flagged that they cannot both be met within it as planned.

## Expected

- **Two CandidateAssemblies**, both admissible and both complete enough to propose: different
  beneficiaries (the Society; vulnerable customers), different owners, different authorities,
  different baselines.
- **C contributes to neither candidate's semantics.** It evidences a *relationship*: `unresolved` on
  **each** candidate records the staffing-envelope tension, citing C, and names what would settle it
  — an organisational decision about the envelope, which is a **decision contribution** (C also
  yields an identification lead for a forthcoming consequential decision).
- Both candidates proposed; the tension surfaced to ratification, not adjudicated.

## Must not

- **Resolve the tension** — by preferring the Board's mandate over the efficiency mandate, by
  recency, or by any reading of relative importance. Interpretation holds no authority to trade
  commitments off.
- **Merge into a compromise candidate** ("reduce cost while improving service") that nobody made.
- Suppress C because it fits neither candidate. The relationship is the most valuable finding in the
  fixture.
- Downgrade either candidate's confidence *because* of the conflict — each is well-evidenced; the
  tension is between them, not within them.
