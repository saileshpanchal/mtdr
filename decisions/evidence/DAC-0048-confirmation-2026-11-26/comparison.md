# The adopter's candidates against the sealed key

Made by the maintainer's assistant after the adopter had finished. The key ([`corpus/key/answer-key.md`](corpus/key/answer-key.md))
was written by an author who had never seen MTDR, so it states what an ordinary reader would say
happened, not what the standard would record. Where the two differ, the difference is noted, not
scored.

**Where the standard and an ordinary reader part company.**
- **D1 and D2.** The key names a forum as the decision-maker, while the standard leaves the owner
  missing unless an individual is named. This is the semantic roles working as written, not an error.
- **D4's owner.** The adopter recorded the joint act as a conflict between two owners. That overstates
  it, as the adopter says (friction item 12); the standard has no form for a joint owner.
- **D4's authority.** The Chair's act ("Agreed, go with 1 October") does not cite 4.5. The adopter
  used the specification's broad reading: an instrument named in "material shown to have been before
  whoever acted". Under the narrow reading in TDR-0049 and both recovery skills ("the act cites the
  instrument"), the authority is missing, and 4.5 is an applicability finding that records the
  Company Secretary's advice. The key, written by a lay reader, gives 4.5. Both readings keep the
  evidence; they differ on whether it fills the `authority` role. The adopter also found that the
  broad reading cannot show its own condition in a candidate: `considered` is allowed only on
  context roles (friction item 18).

## What the comparison does not show

It compares outcomes, not method. It does not check that each fragment's grade or evidence kind is
right beyond the validator's pass, and it does not test whether a different adopter would reach the
same four candidates. One run, by one agent, over one corpus of nine documents.
