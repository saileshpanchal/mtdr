### Five rulings

| # | Ruling | Required change |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Authority by fit | A delegation instrument may establish applicable authority, but does not prove the decision-maker relied on it. | Record evidenced applicability separately from historical invocation. Never invent an authority contribution. |
| 2. Three resolutions, one vote | One composite candidate where the evidence establishes a single authoritative act. | Preserve the three resolutions as individually traceable consequences. Separate votes or decisions warrant separate candidates. |
| 3. Delegation scheme adoption | Conditional. A contemporaneous authenticated instrument may itself constitute an act of adoption. | Do not reject solely because the evidence is the adopted instrument. Require evidence of its legal or organisational effect. |
| 4. Temporary becomes permanent | Continued operation does not establish a decision to make an arrangement permanent. | Record persistence as observed behaviour; flag missing authority for continuation. Recover a new decision only if evidenced. |
| 5. Single-source grouping | Approve an explicit singleton grouping basis. | Amend shared grouping semantics through the appropriate governing TDR, with regression fixtures. |

### The same-day correction

> A minute documenting a meeting may be evidence of an event that occurred that day, even if the
> minute was written afterwards. However, two documents sharing a calendar date does not establish
> their chronological relationship.
>
> The specification should distinguish event time, source creation time and interpretation time,
> preserving indeterminate ordering where the evidence cannot resolve it.
