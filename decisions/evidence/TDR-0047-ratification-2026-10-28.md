The reviewer accepted, across the rounds, that:

1. **Permission to implement is not permission to declare proven.** The implementation exists to
   generate the evidence; it does not determine the evidential standard.
2. **The evidence is frozen before the implementation** — the pre-registration criteria, the scenario
   corpus, and the evaluator's specification, in that order, before any OIR content.
3. **The measure is warranted organisational knowledge**, tested by replacing a participant and asking
   five operational questions with and without the OIR — never textual similarity.
4. **Uncertainty can fail a reconstruction alone**: invented authority fails even when the proposed
   action is reasonable.
5. **Possibility decides; cost does not.** Reconstruction effort is a secondary observation and may
   never become an admission criterion after the results are seen.
6. **A lifecycle test proves capability-relativity, not nativeness.**
7. **Every run names five pins** — pre-OIR standard, OIR implementation, corpus, evaluator, runtime.
8. **The independent reviewer** may reject the native-state claim even when the implementation works,
   and conducts an evidential challenge, not a code review.
9. **The experiment is open source**, so it is published on `main` for review, experimental and
   excluded from the standard's counts, bundles and claims.
10. A projection outcome is **a legitimate result, not a failure**.
