# Changelog

All notable changes to the TDR standard (and its MTDR markdown reference format) are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/), and the standard adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). The *reasoning* behind each significant change lives in [`/decisions`](decisions/) as a TDR — this file is the index to it.

## [1.28.0] — 2026-08-18

### Added
- **Runtime evidence, kept as an artefact** ([`skills/packaging/runtime-evidence.md`](skills/packaging/runtime-evidence.md)) — five runtimes checked against their own documentation, each row recording discovery mechanism, canonical-skill compatibility, progressive-disclosure behaviour, invocation, transformations required, source, date and **what could not be verified**. Research embedded invisibly in an adapter cannot be re-checked when a runtime changes; research recorded as evidence can. Three rows rest on primary sources; two say plainly that they do not, rather than being quietly promoted to complete the matrix.
- **The falsification criterion** ([TDR-0038](decisions/TDR-0038-distribution-bundles-and-the-layer-boundary.md)) — *if supporting a runtime requires changing the meaning of a canonical MTDR artefact, that runtime is unsupported; it is not a reason to change MTDR.* Adapters translate **packaging and invocation, never semantics**.
- **Generated distribution bundles** ([`mtdr_packaging/`](mtdr_packaging/), [`skills/packaging/`](skills/packaging/)) — the catalogue is enumerated from each `package.yaml`, never hand-maintained, and the suite proves the bundles are neither stale, nor divergent, nor semantically altered. **One collection, not one per surface**: every runtime verified reads canonical `SKILL.md` files directly, so five copies would prove only that copying works — a single collection every surface points at makes a divergent catalogue *impossible* rather than merely detectable.
- **Every runtime checked is `direct`.** Not one requires a transformed skill file. The strongest available evidence for TDR-0024 turns out to be that there is almost nothing for a packaging adapter to do — and the reason is the frontmatter discipline: the Agent Skills specification admits six fields, MTDR's contract permits two, and a field outside the spec makes packaging fail hard. The narrowest surface is the most portable one, and the suite has enforced it since long before any of this was checked.
- **The layer boundary, codified** ([`specification/standards-boundary.md`](specification/standards-boundary.md) §layers) — knowledge and provenance packaging · procedural capability (`SKILL.md`) · packaging and distribution · interaction (MCP) · **governed semantics and lifecycle (MTDR)** · **standing (authorised human acts)**. Two rows carry the argument: **nothing but MTDR occupies the semantics-and-lifecycle layer** — a skill expresses a method but cannot say that a commitment lacking a counter-signatory is a valid candidate and an ineligible ratification — and **nothing at all occupies the standing layer**, because a tool that appears to supply standing has relocated accountability into software. An adopter does not choose between MTDR and their agent stack; they keep the stack and gain the layer it lacks.

- **The adoption guide** ([`practice/using-mtdr-with-your-framework.md`](practice/using-mtdr-with-your-framework.md)) — runtime-neutral, written from the adopter's side, and built on one inversion: **your framework is source material, not something MTDR is configured to match.** You do not adapt record semantics to your terminology or write your governance model into a prompt; MTDR reads what your material says and reconstructs candidates from it, preserving provenance, gaps, conflicts and uncertainty. If your framework genuinely needs semantics the standard lacks, that is a proposal through the language admission test, not a customisation. The guide is honest about the first run being uncomfortable — decisions with no owner, conflicts nobody knew about, figures that trace to nothing, decisions nobody wrote down — and about that discomfort being the product.
- **The Brackwell adoption case** ([`tests/adoption/`](tests/adoption/)) — a complete fictional local-authority framework: a scheme of delegation, a Cabinet minute, a business case with a measured baseline and a counter-signature, a capital schedule that disagrees with it, a programme board that quietly narrowed the scope, and an operations note where a workaround has become the way things work. [FIX-301](tests/adoption/FIX-301-brackwell-reconstruction.md) states the expected reconstruction and the eight things it must never do — inventing an owner for a committee decision, resolving the date conflict, recomputing the saving, drawing attribution from an observation, adjudicating an authority question, or **adding local-government semantics to the standard**. Deliberately not financial services: every other fictional case here is a bank, and "this is an organisational language, not a banking ontology" is better demonstrated than repeated.
- **A progressive-disclosure budget** ([`tests/adoption/disclosure.py`](tests/adoption/disclosure.py)) — because *"works in five runtimes"* must not secretly mean *"load the entire repository into context"*. Measured in three stages against the corpus: **catalogue alone 1.3%**, plus the most demanding single skill's body, plus all the references that skill names — **5.0%**. Selecting and running one skill costs a twentieth of loading everything, and the suite fails if either figure breaks its ceiling.

### Changed
- Collecting the skills into a flat directory breaks their canonical relative links, and **they are not rewritten** — rewriting them would modify a canonical artefact, which the criterion forbids. The bundle requires the reference-knowledge layer to be deployed alongside, and the suite exempts the byte-identical copies from the link check because the originals are checked and the copies are proved identical. The finding underneath is recorded rather than smoothed over: **for MTDR the natural distribution unit is a subset of the repository, not a flat directory of skill files**, because TDR-0023 deliberately puts skills next to the semantics they serve.

## [1.27.0] — 2026-08-18

### Added
- **The derivation projection and `show-me`** ([TDR-0036](decisions/TDR-0036-derivation-projection-and-show-me.md), [`specification/derivation-projection.md`](specification/derivation-projection.md), [`schemas/shared/derivation-projection.schema.json`](schemas/shared/derivation-projection.schema.json), [`skills/shared/show-me/`](skills/shared/show-me/)) — a defined, versioned, machine-comparable answer to the question a reader asks first: **why does this record say that?** The skill's one rule is the whole of it: **it projects what was recorded and never constructs what was not.** Not explanation generation, not retrieval-augmented answering, not interpretability tooling that reconstructs a plausible account — every one of those produces text where evidence is absent, which is exactly the failure a governed record cannot tolerate. A value with no chain behind it is reported as unsupported, and where the chain is broken, the break *is* the answer.
- **A view, not a fourth interchange structure.** Computed from SourceFragment, RecordContribution and CandidateAssembly, carrying nothing they do not; TDR-0022's three levels are untouched. That is what keeps it honest — there is nothing in a projection that could be authored, only things that could be found.
- **Five states, and no sixth**: `supported · inferred · conflicting · missing · unsupported`. No `likely`, no `weakly-supported`, no `plausible`, and **no aggregate provenance score at any level** — each would be a fresh judgement made at projection time, and a projection that grades its own evidence has stopped reporting and started assessing. Recorded confidences travel through unchanged; nothing new is computed. **`missing` and `unsupported` are constitutionally distinct**: both have an empty chain, and the difference is whether anything declared the gap. The schema makes it mechanical — `missing` requires a declaration and forbids a rendered value; `unsupported` requires a rendered value and forbids a declaration — because it is the easiest collapse to make and the most costly, since unsupported content is indistinguishable from supported content by reading.
- **"I don't know why" is a valid machine-readable result.** A projection that is largely `missing` and `unsupported` has not failed; it has reported the state of the organisation's evidence. A broken derivation chain is a finding, not a defect to be patched with generated prose.
- **The projection is context-free; validation is not.** Whether a fragment is presently reachable is relational validity's question under TDR-0033. The projection reports *what was recorded*; the validation result reports *what can currently be established*. Keeping them apart is what lets two participants' projections of the same records be compared field by field, since nothing in a projection depends on what either could reach.
- **Twelve fixtures** ([`tests/inspectability/`](tests/inspectability/)) — five states the contract must accept, seven probes it must reject: the retrospective justification, a sixth state, the provenance score, the filled gap, the suggested value, a contribution with no fragment behind it, and a projection conferring standing. The synthetic subjects are a fictional **local authority** rather than a bank, deliberately: every other fictional case in this repository is financial services, and "this is an organisational language, not a banking ontology" is better demonstrated than asserted.

- **The obligation chain, named and computed** ([TDR-0037](decisions/TDR-0037-release-eligibility-from-standing-governance.md), [`specification/obligation-chain.md`](specification/obligation-chain.md)) — **Decision → Constraint → Obligation → Evidence → Current state → Eligibility for next transition**, claimed at its actual scope: the mechanism MTDR *currently uses* to decide whether a governed transition is permissible from available evidence, and **not** a universal organisational ontology. `released` becomes a governed transition of this repository, and `tests/verify.py` requests it on every run with eligibility **derived from the obligations register rather than a curated checklist** — `implementation: pending` contributes `fail`, `verification: pending | release-gated` contributes `indeterminate`, `fail` outranks `indeterminate`, and every contributing obligation is named. This exercises TDR-0033's fourth dimension for the first time; until now every result reported `not-applicable`, and a dimension that is always inapplicable is one nobody has tested.
- **The strict definition, which is the load-bearing part**: *eligibility answers whether recorded evidence satisfies already-authorised transition conditions; it does not decide what those conditions ought to be.* Hence the division of labour — decision establishes condition → obligation represents condition → evidence addresses obligation → validator computes satisfaction → eligibility reports result → **authorised participant decides and acts**. Every step before the last is mechanical; the last is not, and never becomes so. That is what keeps MTDR from quietly becoming the authority it exists to model. **Eligibility is not authority**: a `pass` means the request may be *placed before* whoever decides it, and a must-not probe now proves a result cannot carry a release-performing or authority field.
- **The same shape, twice** — candidate → obligations/evidence → eligible for ratification → human act → standing, and pre-release → obligations/evidence → eligible for release → authorised act → released. Recorded as an invariant to preserve rather than a coincidence: a record family that invents its own readiness mechanism has diverged for no reason.
- **The repository currently reports itself *not eligible*** for the release its own accepted assurance cases defined, naming DAC-0032 constraint 2 — detached records cannot yet identify their applicable specification version. That is the mechanism working, and a gate whose first honest output is "not eligible" is more trustworthy than one that opened green.

### Changed
- The suite now runs both contract-fixture families through one runner, and checks that **stated skill counts match the skills on disk** — the count had drifted silently to twenty-four, and a number in prose that nothing checks is an unverified claim about the repository's own contents.
- The repository result now carries a real `requested_transition`, so its exit status and its eligibility are visibly different things: nothing is failing, and the repository is not yet eligible. Both statements are true at once, which is the point of reporting dimensions rather than a verdict.

## [1.26.0] — 2026-08-17

### Added
- **The standards boundary** ([TDR-0035](decisions/TDR-0035-standards-boundary.md), [`specification/standards-boundary.md`](specification/standards-boundary.md)) — fifteen external standards and projects, each with a reasoned disposition from `adopt | map | interoperate | defer | reject` **and an explicit statement of what that disposition does not mean**. A register of one-word verdicts would be read as endorsement; the foreclosure column is where the boundary actually lives, and `tests/verify.py` rejects a row that is a bare verdict. The load-bearing distinction is **adopt versus interoperate**: *everything MTDR adopts, an adopter must also have*, which is why the adopt column holds three entries — JSON Schema, `SKILL.md` and portable markdown. Structural ideas borrowed from elsewhere, such as in-toto's subject-and-versioned-predicate envelope, are **mapped, not adopted**: taking a shape costs an adopter nothing, and calling it adoption would misstate what they need. Nothing else may become a dependency without an entry saying so — no graph, policy engine, agent platform, transport or machine-identity system is required to interpret, validate or produce a conformant record.
- **The SHACL spike, executed rather than assumed** ([`spikes/shacl-relational-validity/`](spikes/shacl-relational-validity/)) — four real MTDR relational constraints written as shapes and run under pyshacl 0.40.1. Lineage resolution and DAC-to-record binding express beautifully in core SHACL (`sh:class`, `sh:node`); version compatibility and package closure need `sh:sparql`, at which point the shape is a SPARQL query in a Turtle string. **The decisive finding is neither: SHACL cannot express `indeterminate`.** In SHACL the data graph is the world, so a reference to a burnt identifier that will never exist and a reference to a real record held in an unreachable distribution produce the *identical* violation — and MTDR requires `fail` for one and `indeterminate` for the other (FIX-104, FIX-103). The severity ladder does not rescue it: `sh:Warning` is an authoring-time choice, not a runtime epistemic state. Adopting SHACL would collapse `indeterminate` into `fail`, which DAC-0033 identifies as making offline and zero-install use impractical, and would install a graph as a normative dependency that TDR-0033 explicitly declines. **Disposition: map, not adopt** — SHACL entered the register as a spike and left it as a map, because it was run.
- **What the spike did establish, kept** — partition by the declared evaluation context *first*, then validate. Withhold what the context cannot reach, report it `indeterminate`, and hand SHACL only the part of the world it is entitled to close over: the same graph and shapes then give the correct answer, because the judgement SHACL cannot make was made before it ran. Demonstrated in the spike's own run. That makes SHACL a sound component under MTDR's context declaration and a sound export target for a graph-native adopter — and not the contract.

### Changed
- `decisions/ALLOCATION.md`: TDR-0035 allocated by this commit, TDR-0036 next free. Allocation in the same commit as the record, as TDR-0034 requires.

## [1.25.0] — 2026-08-17

### Added
- **The obligations register** ([`decisions/evidence/DAC-0032-0033-obligations.yaml`](decisions/evidence/DAC-0032-0033-obligations.yaml)) — all fifteen constraints from the two cases accepted `proceed-with-constraints`, each with owner, measure, the evidence satisfying it, and a note saying what is actually true. **Two independent fields, not one conflated state**: `implementation: pending | implemented | not-applicable` and `verification: pending | verified | release-gated | not-applicable`. Implementation and verification are different dimensions, and a requirement can be fully implemented today and intentionally unverified until release; one combined state would force a choice between claiming a proof that does not exist and hiding work that does. **`release-gated` is not a synonym for pending** — it means the proof is defined and owned and cannot be run in this repository state, so at the first real release these become executable acceptance tests rather than prose reminders. Current standing: 10 verified, 4 release-gated, 1 pending.
- **The register is checked, not merely written** — `tests/verify.py` parses the constraint numbering out of both accepted DACs and requires exact correspondence. A constraint cannot silently regress, a case cannot gain a constraint that goes unregistered, an id cannot be invented, evidence must resolve, and `verified` with no evidence is rejected.
- **DAC-0033 constraint 6 is now enforced rather than promised.** The suite asserts the VR schema still globally requires `finance_countersignatory` — the very contradiction TDR-0033 identified — so an opportunistic fix trips a check and is routed to TDR-0019 instead of landing quietly. A constraint to leave something alone turns out to be testable.

## [1.24.0] — 2026-08-17

### Added
- **The validation-result contract** ([`specification/validation-result.md`](specification/validation-result.md), [`schemas/validation-result.schema.json`](schemas/validation-result.schema.json)) — the versioned contract TDR-0033 requires. Every result names its **TDR-0032 conformance subject** and the **specification identity and version** it was assessed against, both first-class required fields rather than prose conventions; reports **structural · semantic · relational · lifecycle-transition eligibility** independently, all four always present, exactly once each; and uses the vocabulary `pass | fail | indeterminate | not-applicable`, where `fail`, `indeterminate` and `not-applicable` each require a reason. **Two of TDR-0032's seven subjects are deliberately inadmissible**: `collection`, because DAC-0032 constraint 6 forbids advertising conformance for a class with no contract — a result that cannot be governed cannot be issued — and `participant`, because execution is a behavioural claim and TDR-0033 is explicit that behavioural conformance is not a validation dimension. Every remaining subject has a serialised representation, which is why structural `not-applicable` is unreachable and rejected.
- **The object is closed at every level**, so what a result may never contain is rejected structurally rather than discouraged in prose: **no aggregate verdict** (`valid`, `conformant`, `overall`, `score` — DAC-0033 constraint 8), **no standing** (`ratified`, `accepted`, `authority` — TDR-0027), **no repair** (`corrected`, `suggested_value`), **no inferred value**. A field that could be read alone would be read alone.
- **[`mtdr_validation/`](mtdr_validation/) — an explicitly non-normative reference implementation.** A conforming independent implementation is governed by the specification and the schema, **never by behavioural equivalence with this code**; if they disagree, the code is wrong. It has no `is_valid()`, no truthiness and no exit-code helper, for the same reason the schema has no verdict field. The distinction is the one JSON Schema itself draws between a validation vocabulary and any particular validator's output, and it matters directly for the recovery experiment, where independent runtimes are not being measured against this Python.
- **Sixteen validation fixtures** ([`tests/validation/`](tests/validation/)) — eight the contract must accept, eight it must reject, each with a must-not section and a synthetic subject in [`synthetic/`](tests/validation/synthetic/). Three pairs carry the weight, because each is separated by a single fact that is easy to lose: **unreachable vs absent** (an unavailable reference is `indeterminate`, never `fail` — failing it would make offline and zero-install use impossible, passing it would conceal real inconsistency); **ineligible vs unknown**; **inapplicable vs unestablished**. The must-not probes cover the aggregate verdict, conferred standing, the collection subject, the omitted dimension, the skipped-but-labelled-inapplicable check, eligibility without a requested transition, the unexplained `indeterminate`, and the helpful validator that suggests the value it found missing.
- **Classified normative closure** in the normative package manifests (TDR-0032, DAC-0032 constraint 3) — every dependency classified `normative | conformance | provenance | explanatory | navigation | historical` with a stated reason. **This is role metadata, not a six-level trust hierarchy**: historical material can be authoritative evidence about what occurred while being non-normative about current behaviour, which matters increasingly as superseded and burnt records accumulate. Only `normative` entries constrain extraction, and explanatory hyperlinks do not become normative merely by existing. The suite proves the closure is *complete* — no outward reference a package makes may go undeclared.

### Changed
- **`tests/verify.py` no longer prints `conforming — all checks passed`.** That untyped aggregate green verdict was a live breach of DAC-0032 constraint 1 and DAC-0033 constraint 8 in the repository's own tooling. The run now ends with a **repository-subject result** reporting the four dimensions independently, and states plainly that it implies nothing about any record, package, skill or participant inside the repository, and that **the exit status is an operational signal about the run, not a conformance judgement**. The suite is now a *consumer* of the contract rather than its implementation.
- New checks: the validation fixtures accepted and rejected as published; **no untyped conformance claim** on a normative surface (the phrase may be quoted by the decisions that prohibit it, never asserted); classified closure with no undeclared outward reference; must-not probes over the reference implementation, including byte comparison proving the assessed object is unmodified; and a **serialisation round-trip** — a result written, reloaded in a fresh process, and yielding the same interpretation. Each new invariant was exercised against a synthetic violation before being accepted.

### Still open, deliberately
- **DAC-0033 constraint 3** — the incomplete Value Record proving case (a candidate lacking only its finance counter-signature passing current-state validity and failing ratification eligibility) waits on TDR-0019 remediating the VR schema. DAC-0033 constraint 6 forbids fixing that schema opportunistically, so FIX-106 proves the *shape* on a decision record and says explicitly that it is not the proof.
- **DAC-0032 constraints 2, 4 and 5** — the version-bound detached record, the zero-install trial by a participant unknown to the author, and the registration/export round-trip are release gates and remain unproved. The serialisation round-trip added here is a local precursor to the first and claims nothing about external interoperability.

## [1.23.0] — 2026-08-17

### Added
- **Public allocation authority, and allocation is irreversible** ([TDR-0034](decisions/TDR-0034-public-allocation-authority.md)) — this repository governs the `TDR-*` and `DAC-*` namespaces. Private repositories govern only their own, and **no normative mirror may exist**: two records of one truth is precisely the drift this standard exists to eliminate. The public sequence had been governed by a register held in a private repository, sixteen identifiers stale, which a contributor could not consult — every condition for the TDR-0031 collision present by design, and a direct violation of [TDR-0020](decisions/TDR-0020-open-organisational-record-repository-boundary.md)'s requirement that this repository be usable without knowing that any particular consumer exists. This record **repairs an implementation that was violating an existing boundary**; it does not establish a new one. The second ruling: **once a TDR identifier has been materially published into repository history, it is never reassigned, irrespective of whether the proposed record subsequently gains standing.** TDR-0031 ruled that reservation confers no standing; that does not license recycling. **Standing** is whether a record is constitutionally recognised, and is governed. **Identity** is whether an identifier has already been used to refer to a particular attempted judgement, and is acquired on publication, permanently. *History may contain failed propositions; it must never contain ambiguous identities.*
- **[`decisions/ALLOCATION.md`](decisions/ALLOCATION.md)** — the normative, human-readable allocation register: committed state, correct the instant the commit lands. TDR-0001–0027 and 0031–0033 allocated; **TDR-0028, 0029 and 0030 burnt** — allocated on a dropped branch, address without standing, never reusable; TDR-0034 allocated by the commit that introduces the register; TDR-0035 next free. Allocation occurs **in the same commit that introduces the record**, because any gap between allocation and artefact is a window in which they can diverge.
- **Machine-enforced allocation invariants** (`tests/verify.py`) — defined **by state**, since burnt identifiers deliberately have no governed representation: `allocated` → exactly one governed representation; `burnt` → none, ever; every governed representation → exactly one allocated entry; plus duplicate-`id` detection. **Next free is derived, not authoritative** — the durable truth is which identifiers are allocated or burnt, and the register's stated next free is *proved* equal to `max(allocated ∪ burnt) + 1` rather than trusted, because a hand-maintained "next free" is itself a drift opportunity. Each invariant was exercised against a synthetic violation before the check was accepted.

## [1.22.0] — 2026-08-16

### Added
- **Human ratification confers standing** ([TDR-0027](decisions/TDR-0027-human-ratification-confers-standing.md), [DAC-0027](decisions/DAC-0027-human-ratification-confers-standing.md)) — standing originates in an authorised human act; MTDR represents it as `accepted` on evidence, and never manufactures it.
- **The constitutional reconciliation of an identifier collision** ([TDR-0031](decisions/TDR-0031-reconcile-ratified-judgement-after-identifier-collision.md), [DAC-0031](decisions/DAC-0031-reconcile-ratified-judgement-after-identifier-collision.md)) — a ratified judgement's candidate identifier collided with a different accepted record. The rule: **standing originates in the authorised human act, attaches to the substantive judgement, and is merely *represented* through repository identity. Repository identity is not the source of standing.** A collision neither invalidates ratification nor authorises replacing the accepted occupant. A fresh prospective representation may carry the earlier standing only where a named human confirms four conditions — judgement, constraints, exclusions and scope unchanged; original ratification evidence immutable and attributable; the new representation identifies the collided source and why a fresh identifier was necessary; and an evidence crosswalk binds candidate, ratification act and new representation. If any substantive proposition changes it is no longer reconciliation but a new decision needing new ratification. Also ruled: **identifier reservation confers neither standing nor ownership — a number is merely an address until a governed representation exists.**
- **Typed multi-object conformance** ([TDR-0032](decisions/TDR-0032-typed-multi-object-conformance.md), [DAC-0032](decisions/DAC-0032-typed-multi-object-conformance.md)) — conformance is always claimed by a defined subject (record · interchange structure · package · skill · participant/implementation · collection · repository) against the specification applicable to it. **There is no untyped normative claim that something is merely "MTDR conformant".** Conformance at one level implies nothing at another. The smallest independent record claim is a **version-bound record**; a package's **normative closure** contains or durably identifies every dependency and compatible version needed to interpret and validate it. The register becomes an **optional collection and governance mechanism**, not a prerequisite for conformance and not a source of standing.
- **Four-dimensional validity** ([TDR-0033](decisions/TDR-0033-four-dimensional-validity.md), [DAC-0033](decisions/DAC-0033-four-dimensional-validity.md)) — validation reports **structural · semantic · relational · lifecycle-transition eligibility** as separate dimensions with explicit dependencies, **not a success pipeline**, each with reasons, evidence, applicable specification and context. The common vocabulary is `pass | fail | indeterminate | not-applicable`, where **`indeterminate` is not a softer fail and never permits inferring the missing fact**. Validity is relative to the current state: information required only for a later transition must not make a state in which it is legitimately absent invalid.
- **Permanent reconciliation evidence** (`decisions/evidence/`) — ratification records, governance ruling, and source/target crosswalks for TDR-0032 and TDR-0033. These are **permanent evidence, not migration scaffolding**: they explain why the later identifiers legitimately represent earlier ratified human acts. The human review recorded there confirms **semantic equivalence only — it does not re-ratify** the substantive judgements, whose standing originates in the earlier acts.

### Changed
- `TDR-0012` status → `superseded`, and **narrowly**: TDR-0032 displaces only its register-as-universal-minimum judgement and explicitly retains the rest. **Supersession is judgement-granular, not file-granular** (TDR-0032 §110, DAC-0032 constraint 7); TDR-0012 remains immutable historical evidence.
- Public TDR-0020 and TDR-0025 are unchanged. TDR-0028/0029/0030 were allocated to proposed records on a branch subsequently dropped; they gain no standing from the repair and are never reused — the public allocation register introduced at 1.23.0 records them as burnt.

## [1.21.0] — 2026-08-10

### Added
- **`recover-value-records`** ([`records/value/skills/recover-value-records/`](records/value/skills/recover-value-records/)) — the value language's **composition skill** and user-facing entry point, under TDR-0026's composition ruling: it runs identify → reconcile → draft → challenge → validate in order, hands each stage's output to the next unmodified, routes split/merge dispositions back through reconciliation with the challenge's reasons, and stops at the ratification boundary. **It orchestrates and decides nothing** — every semantic rule lives in the stage skill that owns it, and any instruction in the entry point that changes *what* a stage decides rather than *when it runs* is a defect. Its one rule is the experiment's: **no stage is allowed to invent the missing transition.** The report it produces is bounded deliberately: candidates with completeness views and dispositions, what remains unknown, what remains conflicting, what was unclassified, what was found and not used — and nothing else; portfolio views, prioritisation and organisational judgement sit above the artefact boundary with whatever consumes the records.

### Changed
- Skill count references updated to twenty-four; the value package manifest gains its `orchestration` entry; the package README names the entry point.

## [1.20.0] — 2026-08-10

### Changed
- **VR-2: the five value skills tightened to exact contracts.** `identify-value-contributions` gains its contract — material + metadata in; fragments and contributions out, each carrying contribution class, `derivation` and temporal context; **"no value contribution identified" is successful execution**; the `unclassified` outcome reported rather than near-fitted (the anti-pattern now names the near-fit as role-invention's quieter twin). `reconcile-value-contributions` outputs **candidate groupings with reasons** and the seven findings — reinforcing · contradictions · different beneficiaries · temporal evolution · supersession · unresolved ambiguity · competing propositions — with the "best bank for Britain" family as the worked may-or-may-not-be-one case. `draft-value-record` carries the discipline verbatim — *populate only what the evidence supports; missing means missing; inferred means inferred; conflicting means conflicting* — and emits the completeness view, state-only. `challenge-value-record` carries the full twelve-question set (aspiration vs commitment, activity vs value, KPI vs value, the displacing measure, unauthorised reconciliation, currency, falsification evidence beyond the candidate's own fragments) and closes with **exactly one of five dispositions per candidate: split · merge · reject · incomplete · retain** — what makes it more useful than generic self-criticism. `validate-value-record` gains checks 7–8: **contribution coverage and unsupported claims** (the unsupported-claim list feeds the benchmark's unsupported assertion rate; unused contributions listed, since silently dropped evidence is how challenge material disappears) and **temporal consistency**.

## [1.19.0] — 2026-08-10

### Added
- **The value-recovery vocabulary** ([TDR-0026](decisions/TDR-0026-value-recovery-vocabulary.md), [`records/value/specification/value-contribution.md`](records/value/specification/value-contribution.md)) — thirteen contribution classes defining what a fragment can contribute **without constituting a Value Record**: beneficiary · commitment · success · measure · constraint · assumption · time · priority · protection · trade-off · challenge · supersession · context. Interpretive vocabulary carried through the existing `RecordContribution` structure — **not another schema** — and three of the classes (trade-off, challenge, context) deliberately never populate a record field, because losing them loses exactly the disagreement and uncertainty recovery exists to preserve. Boundary tests with worked contrasts hold the edges: value ≠ decision ≠ activity ≠ KPI ≠ generic aspiration.
- **The `unclassified` recovery outcome** — the classes are the *current* normative vocabulary, not a claim of completeness. Material relevant to Value that fits no class is reported as unclassified (fragment refs, what was recognised, why classification failed) — a reporting outcome, **not a fourteenth role**. A recurring unclassified pattern is evidence for a superseding decision, never a forced fit: the no-invention rule applied to the vocabulary itself.
- **`derivation: explicit | inferred`** — optional field on the shared `RecordContribution` interchange structure. Record-neutral, so substrate-level; additive, so every prior instance still validates (verified with negative case).
- **The completeness view** — per mandatory role, `supported | partial | missing | conflicting`, plus the one-sentence organisational reading. **State only, deliberately**: derivation is visible through the supporting contributions and never folded into the grades, so an inferred proposition cannot present as complete and an incomplete one cannot hide behind "inferred".
- **Eight new fixtures, and the class layout** ([`records/value/fixtures/`](records/value/fixtures/)) — fixtures reorganised onto two axes the documentation refuses to flatten: six **value classes** (quantified · qualitative · normative · protective · environmental-social · conflicting) and three **recovery conditions** (incomplete · superseded · no-value). New: FIX-010 (the scattered SME proposition — aspiration hardened by later authority, constrained, beneficiary-bounded, then quietly superseded under continuing Board lineage); FIX-011 (a KPI pack yields measures and **no candidate** — a number nobody committed to is the shadow of a claim); FIX-012 (a programme paper is activity, not value, and its board approval routes to the decision language); FIX-013 (a £3.2m NPV headline displacing an access-outcome proposition — the figure is evidence *about* the commitment, never the commitment); FIX-014 (three wordings, one commitment — convergence on named identity evidence, with a near-identically-worded fourth statement that must stay out, and "semantic similarity" as a grouping basis failing even when the grouping is right); FIX-015 (the completeness view working end to end — committed, unmeasured, unreviewed, proposed anyway); FIX-016 (the unclassified outcome exercised — option value fitting no class, with `protection` considered and rejected); FIX-017 (an authorised trade-off recorded as a weighing the organisation performed, never as a conflict and never re-performed by interpretation).

### Noted
- The six-class taxonomy supersedes the earlier "PCA" working label; **protective value** expresses that class directly. Value is not synonymous with incremental benefit — organisations also value what they are explicitly trying not to lose.

## [1.14.0] — 2026-08-10

### Added
- **Value Record v2.0.0 — the Operational Value Commitment specified** ([TDR-0019](decisions/TDR-0019-value-record-v2-operational-value-commitment.md)). `spec-value-record.md` had stood at 1.3.0 through twelve releases, the only specification that had not moved since it shipped, and its footer claimed it "versions with `spec.md`" — true at 1.3.0 and false ever since. **This is not a redesign of value.** It is the specification catching up with semantics already discovered, and already encoded experimentally as roles in the record package.
- **The semantic centre stated: an accountable pursuit, not a prediction.** The record does not claim an outcome will occur; it records that a named person committed the organisation to pursuing or preserving one. A commitment can be honoured and the outcome not arrive; a commitment can be abandoned and the outcome arrive anyway. Neither makes the record wrong, and a specification that cannot express them is measuring forecasting accuracy rather than accountability.
- **Eight semantic roles** — *Under · Who commits · For whom · We commit · From · To · By · We will know* — as **roles to be inferred, not a mandatory sentence grammar**. No record is required to phrase itself this way, and real material almost never does; looking for the words rather than the roles is how a skill misses a commitment stated plainly in ordinary prose.
- **`beneficiary` required, and identity-bearing** (`spec-value-record.md` §4.3) — not descriptive metadata. Two commitments identical in value, baseline, measure, timing and wording, differing only in who the outcome is for, are **two commitments**. This is justified empirically rather than by assertion: [`conformance/FIX-003`](records/value/fixtures/FIX-003-two-commitments-not-one.md) supplies exactly that pair, written to one template by one author in one document, where the beneficiary clause is the *only* separating signal. It is consequently the one mandatory role whose absence blocks admission outright rather than being reported as a gap — every other missing role yields an incomplete but useful candidate, while a beneficiary-less one cannot be told apart from a different commitment, making every downstream grouping and supersession unsafe.
- **Two independent state axes.** `status` (`candidate → ratified → settled → superseded`) records what is known about the **record**; `realisation` (`not-started → observing → realised | partially-realised | not-realised | attribution-unresolved | written-off`) records what is known about the **value**. The v1 enum conflated them, which made the two most useful things a value record can say unsayable: *properly ratified and the value has not arrived*, and *the outcome was observed and nobody can yet say we caused it*.
- **Three kinds of evidence, labelled** — **existence** (the commitment was made), **observation** (an outcome occurred), **attribution** (this commitment is why). Collapsing them is the most common failure in benefits practice. **Measurement is not attribution**: a cost line falling next to a programme name is evidence of the second and none of the third, so `attribution-unresolved` is a legitimate settlement rather than an incomplete record. A standard unable to express it forces every observed movement to be claimed or discarded.
- **`committed_by` and `authority` required** — who commits the organisation, and what the commitment rests on. Both named or traceable; a committee cannot commit, it can only endorse someone who does.
- **Consequential change supersedes** (§7.1) — a change to beneficiary, intended outcome, timing, funding or protected value changes *what was committed to*. Revising a measurement method or baseline source is an in-place lifecycle fact. Recording the former as an update destroys the evidence that the organisation changed its mind, which is the one thing the record exists to preserve.
- **[`schema/vr.schema.json`](records/value/schema/vr.schema.json)** — the Value Record's first schema, closing the asymmetry noted at 1.13.0. Three record types, three schemas. It enforces the state separation directly: a `candidate` cannot carry a `realisation` other than `not-started`.

### Changed
- **`spec-value-record.md` → 2.0.0, and it now versions independently of `spec.md`.** The stale "versions with spec.md" footer is corrected rather than left to mislead.
- `templates/vr.md`, `records/vr/*`, `skills/value-record`, `skills/draft-vr`, `skills/validate-record` and `conformance/FIX-001` updated for v2. `spec.md` bumped to 1.14.0, unchanged in substance.
- `examples/example-value-record-practice-skills.md` raised under v2, and its two adapted fields are now *both* flagged — the acting counter-signatory, and an `authority` that resolves to the committer's own decision because this repository has no governing body above its maintainer. An organisation's authority should trace to something the committer does not control, and showing the weakness is more useful than hiding it.

### Breaking
- **This is the first breaking change to any record type in the repository's history, and it is named rather than smoothed over.** `beneficiary`, `committed_by`, `authority` and `realisation` are new required fields and the `status` enum has changed, so **v1 Value Records do not validate against the v2 schema**. Shipping this as a minor version and repeating the customary "every prior record still validates" would have made that claim worthless everywhere else it appears.
- **v1 records remain valid v1 records** and are not retrospectively invalid; they are superseded by a v2 record when materially changed, exactly as the supersession discipline requires. `spec-value-record.md` §8 carries the v1→v2 status mapping, and `validate-record` is required to validate each record against the version it was raised under rather than reporting v1 records as failures.
- TDR and DAC are untouched. All 19 decision records still validate against an unchanged `schema/tdr.schema.json`.

### Noted
- **The counterfactual question is left explicitly open** (§5.2). A commitment is admissible without one; what its absence may prevent is a defensible claim about *incremental* value or attribution — a different thing. Making it mandatory was the tempting option, because it makes incremental value calculable, and it was rejected: it would force organisations to manufacture counterfactual reasoning at commitment time to satisfy a field. Declaring it never required would equally be a settlement the evidence does not support. Interpretation must therefore not report a missing counterfactual as a gap, and must never infer one.

## [1.13.0] — 2026-08-10

### Added
- **Interpretation skills, and the boundary that admits them** ([TDR-0018](decisions/TDR-0018-interpretation-skills-and-the-artefact-boundary.md)) — the thirteen existing skills all assume a human author who already knows the decision. Recognising decisions and value commitments *already made*, scattered across board packs, minutes and business cases, is different work, and it raised a scope question the repository had not answered: `CONTRIBUTING.md` excludes "extraction pipelines", which read at its widest excludes any skill that reads organisational material. TDR-0018 resolves it as a **clarification, not a supersession** — TDR-0001's neutrality boundary stands. The dividing line is **direction, not subject matter**: a vendor-neutral skill that reads material and yields a conformant record *produces* the standard's own output and belongs here; storage, indexing, retrieval, orchestration, graph assembly and persistence *consume* records and remain outside. **The standard ends at the artefact boundary** — its output is portable files, and it has no knowledge of how they are stored, connected, projected or reasoned over.
- **Six interpretation skills** ([`skills/`](skills/)) — `identify-record-contributions` and `reconcile-record-fragments` are record-agnostic; `draft-tdr` and `draft-vr` are per record; `challenge-record` and `validate-record` are one skill each rather than one per record type, because the failures they catch — interpretation unsupported by evidence, gaps quietly filled, conflicts quietly resolved — are properties of *interpretation*, not of any particular record. Only the role list and the admission test differ, and both load from the record package. This is what stops skill proliferation as further records arrive.
- **The interpretation premise, stated as a constraint rather than a claim** — not that records are always scattered, since some sources will carry a complete one, but that **a skill must never *assume* a complete record exists in a single source.** The six-document value commitment is the difficult case the design must support, not a universal fact about organisations.
- **Portable skill interchange structures** ([`shared/`](shared/)) — `SourceFragment` → `RecordContribution` → `CandidateAssembly`. The separation is load-bearing: a structure carrying both the verbatim evidence and the reading of it makes the reading unfalsifiable, because there is nothing left to check it against. Contributions target organisational **objects** (a consequential decision, an Operational Value Commitment) while records are how those objects are *preserved* — so the question asked of material is "does this help reconstruct an OVC?", not "does this document contain Value Record fields?" Carrying `target_object` alongside `target_record_type` is also what keeps the contract extensible to objects whose record status is unsettled. **These are not record types**: they carry no organisational authority, need not be persisted, and are not peers of the record schemas — treating them as such would make this repository a standard for mining pipelines, which TDR-0018 refuses.
- **Contribution is many-to-many.** "The Committee approved the investment subject to maintaining complaints below 4%" is one span contributing to a decision, to an OVC whose review boundary is the threshold, and to the constraint relation between them. A `record_type_hint` on the fragment would have forced one-to-one and lost two of the three readings.
- **Three uncertainties, never collapsed** ([`shared/uncertainty.md`](specification/uncertainty.md)) — `extraction_confidence` (did I read these words correctly), `assembly_confidence` (do these concern the same object) and `epistemic_confidence` (how strong is the evidence for the claim). A model can be entirely confident a document says "£10m saving" while the evidence for the £10m is very weak; merged into one score, that case — the common one in business cases — disappears. The existing `low | medium | high` ladder is reused so values stay comparable; the semantics are namespaced, and no composite is permitted.
- **Proximity is never identity** ([`shared/identity-and-grouping.md`](specification/identity-and-grouping.md)) — grouping is a claim requiring evidence; separation is not. The asymmetry is deliberate: an unmerged pair costs a human two minutes of review, a wrongly merged pair costs the organisation a false record it will act on — and a wrong merge is **indistinguishable from a correct one by inspection**, which no other failure here is. `grouping_basis` is required, same-document and semantic similarity are inadmissible on their own, and the schema enforces it rather than merely documenting it.
- **Candidacy and ratification separated constitutionally** ([`shared/candidacy-and-ratification.md`](specification/candidacy-and-ratification.md)) — a skill may assess whether evidence supports an admissible candidate; it may never turn one into organisational truth. **The skill proposes; the organisation ratifies.** `status` on a candidate assembly is `candidate` and has no other legal value; no skill emits a record beyond `proposed`, assigns an accountable owner as fact, or falls back to a committee where the standard requires an individual. Named to stay clear of [TDR-0017](decisions/TDR-0017-language-admission-test.md)'s seven-criterion *language* admission test — two different admissions in one repository would confuse exactly the participants this is built for.
- **The hindsight rule, reconciled with the existing discipline** — `spec.md` requires context "in the present tense of the decision" and `decision-identification` names **retro-mining** an anti-pattern. Both stand. Reconstructing *reasoning* is forbidden; recovering *contemporaneous evidence* is legitimate, because the reasoning was written down at the time and simply never became a record. The test is that every value traces to material created at the time, and that **outcome knowledge is never evidence**. Where contemporaneous sources are silent the answer is a reported gap, never an inference.
- **Record packages** ([`records/`](records/)) — `tdr/` and `vr/`, each an **overlay rather than a copy**: the specifications, schemas and templates stay at the repository root and the package points at them, because a package holding its own copy would create two sources of truth whose drift stays invisible until a record validates against one and fails the other. Each adds only the semantic roles a contribution may target and a record-specific admissibility test. The extension contract lets a new record type plug in without modifying any skill, structure or existing package — the object and record-type patterns in the contribution schema are open rather than enumerated precisely so that adding one requires no schema change.
- **Commitment and reconciliation separated in the Value Record package** — material contributes to them differently, and mixing them asks an organisation for attribution reasoning before there is anything to attribute. **Measurement is not attribution**: a cost line falling near a project name is evidence a number moved and none that this commitment moved it. So **"outcome observed, attribution unresolved" is a legitimate settlement**, not an incomplete one, and `draft-vr` is required to produce it rather than resolve it. A standard that cannot express that state forces every observed movement to be claimed or discarded.
- **`missing_semantics` as a required field, so that an empty list is a claim rather than a default.** A candidate with missing mandatory roles is still a legitimate candidate — it is an accurate account of what the material supports, and it tells the ratifying owner exactly what to find. The gaps interpretation surfaces most often, a decision with no named individual attached and a benefit with no counter-signatory, are each a reason the record was never going to be defensible.

- **Four known-answer fixtures** ([`conformance/`](conformance/)), each stating what a conforming run must produce **and what it must not** — because producing the right candidate by the wrong route (filling a gap that happens to be correct, merging on similarity where identifiers also agreed) will be wrong and undetectable on real material. FIX-001 assembles a commitment across six sources and requires both gaps reported and a revision recognised as supersession. FIX-002 is a single sentence that must yield **two** candidates. FIX-004 is a source that genuinely carries a complete record, guarding the opposite failure — manufacturing scatter where none exists, or treating a well-formed source as ratification. **FIX-003 matters most**: two workstream benefits identical in value, baseline and wording, differing only in beneficiary, which must not merge. Its failure produces output that looks *better* than a pass — one tidy candidate instead of two — so it can never be judged by appearance. Counts are stated as ranges where span segmentation is a judgement; the number of candidates, the missing-semantics set and every "must not" are exact.
- **Probes 7–10** ([`agents/conformance.md`](tests/conformance/agent-probes.md)), one per fixture, for deployments loading the interpretation skills.

- `spec.md` bumped to 1.13.0. Additive throughout: no field, template or schema change; `schema/` and `templates/` are byte-unchanged, and every prior record still validates — verified, not asserted, against all 18 records.
- Probe count references updated from six to ten in `agents/README.md` and `agents/copilot-studio.md`. The count inside [TDR-0011](decisions/TDR-0011-agent-deployment-pack.md) is deliberately left alone — a record states what was true when it was written, and is superseded rather than edited.

## [1.12.0] — 2026-07-31

### Added
- **A language admission test** ([TDR-0017](decisions/TDR-0017-language-admission-test.md)) — a review asked whether the standard has become a *language* rather than a record format. It has not, and this release publishes the **seven criteria** under which any candidate language may claim otherwise: an abstract syntax, a defined vocabulary, mechanically checkable static rules, speech acts, composition, an interpretation contract, and complete evaluation semantics. The test is stated **language-neutrally** — the same seven questions face any record type that aspires to be a language, and a test written about the thing doing the judging would be worth nothing. This standard is scored against it as its first subject: **one criterion met, two partial, four unmet**. Composition is among the unmet, and is the interesting one: constraint composition has demonstrated a coherent **candidate semantic model** through a tested reference implementation but is not specified by the language, so implementation runs ahead of specification. Stated precisely, that establishes one implementation, one algebra, one interpretation — **not** interoperability, **not** independent convergence, and **not** the composition of conforming records of this standard. [TDR-0016](decisions/TDR-0016-routing-and-disposition-refinements-from-field-application.md) declined a **provenance-identity** relation — how records are produced — which is a different question from what combining two valid records *means*, and so leaves composition unconstrained and TDR-0016 in force. Published *because* the standard currently fails it — a stated test it does not pass is more useful than an unstated one it could be argued into passing.
- **Criterion 7, complete evaluation semantics** — the closure check over the other six, and the strictest: a conforming interpreter can evaluate **every valid statement and relationship the language defines**, using only the published semantics, **without introducing unstated rules**. Criteria 1–6 can all be satisfied while an implementer still has to invent meaning at the first real conflict, leaving two implementations disagreeing while both remain conforming. It is what makes the interpretation contract decidable rather than aspirational. Eight semantic domains must be closed over: applicability, **temporal validity and effectiveness**, supersession, consistency, **conflict, precedence and resolution**, composition, inheritance, and **unresolved or indeterminate states**. Detecting a conflict is not the same as knowing which statement governs, and precedence is not a special case of supersession — supersession replaces a record over time, precedence decides between records that are both standing. Validity and effectiveness are likewise not one property: a statement may exist, be valid, be in force, and be currently applicable — four distinct states, and a language that compresses them cannot express a decision validly made but not yet in force, or in force but not applicable here. The language need not resolve every conflict automatically; it must say which of four outcomes obtains — governed, conflicted, indeterminate, or externally adjudicated. That last domain matters most: completeness does **not** mean every question yields true or false — a language is complete over indeterminacy by defining when a result is unknown, underdetermined, out of scope or contingent on external evidence. A criterion demanding an answer to everything would demand false certainty, and would contradict §11 and the deferral discipline of [TDR-0015](decisions/TDR-0015-visibility-and-effectiveness.md). Criterion 7 is a completeness property *of the semantics*, discharged *by an interpreter*: it does not make a record executable, capture still confers nothing (§10), and it does not establish that two independent implementers would agree — that is a further and later question.
- **Semantic capability and institutional standing kept as independent axes** — what a language can express, and what a conforming interpreter can determine from it, is orthogonal to what standing an organisation has granted it. ("Maturity" is serviceable prose but ambiguous, since the seven criteria are themselves a maturity assessment — of capability, and of nothing else.) A ratified body of records can still be a thin vocabulary, and treating ratification as evidence of completeness is how implementers end up inventing semantics believing the question settled; equally, a complete specification can sit unratified and be discounted for it. The governance ladder by which standing is conferred is an adopter's own business and is not specified here. The criteria are a **commonly observed progression, not a state machine** — these capabilities often emerge in roughly this order but need not: grammar can outrun semantics, semantics can settle without formal grammar, and one abstract syntax can carry several conforming serialisations.
- **Capability scored separately from disposition** — the assessment table reports *what exists* in one column and *why* in another. A capability declined on principle and one merely unbuilt score identically, because a reader assessing what an interpreter can rely on is not helped by knowing which it was. The two-axis discipline, applied inside the test.
- **Authority shown as an orthogonal governance plane** ([`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md)) — the layers table gains a semantic reading (**meaning → memory → reasoning → behaviour**), and authority deliberately does *not* appear as a row in it. A store can preserve records perfectly while enforcing nothing about who was entitled to write them, and TDR-0015's allocation table already splits authority between the store and the downstream consumer. Folding "who may" into "what is held" loses that split.
- **The rule that keeps such a specification inside the boundary**, recorded in the same TDR: **a language specification defines what a conforming interpreter must compute; it does not compute.** Four of the seven criteria otherwise read as demands that the standard resolve authority, compute effectiveness and detect contradiction — which would require abandoning the three-layer contract shipped in v1.10.0. Stated as an obligation on *interpreters*, they do not. The allocation table in [TDR-0015](decisions/TDR-0015-visibility-and-effectiveness.md) stands unchanged, extended by one line: the language defines the meaning of the acts, the store enforces who may perform them, a consumer computes the position — the same split read semantically rather than by enforcement ([`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md)).
- **`rejected` is terminal** (`spec.md` §7) — closing a gap [TDR-0015](decisions/TDR-0015-visibility-and-effectiveness.md) had itself flagged: `rejected` was in the status enum but in no transition rule. A rejected record is never revived, promoted or superseded, because nothing was decided for a later record to replace; the question is reopened by a **new** record citing the rejected one in `derived_from`. No schema change — the enum already carried the value.
- **The abstract-syntax limit, stated plainly** (`spec.md` §11) — the standard defines a serialisation, not a representation-independent model of a record. §6's portable, graph-ready lineage is a real property but a weaker and different one, and the two are regularly conflated. Consequence, written down: conformance is defined against markdown-with-YAML, and a third party cannot write a conforming parser from the specification alone.

### Changed
- `spec.md` bumped to 1.12.0. Additive throughout: no field, template or schema change; `schema/` and `templates/` are byte-unchanged, and every prior record still validates.

## [1.11.0] — 2026-07-24

### Added
- **Routing by materiality, not category** (DAC spec §4) — the first refinements drawn from applying the assurance skills **at scale in the field**, against a corpus of machine-derived, thin decision records. The trigger lists are illustrative, not auto-firing: a lens is routed by whether the decision *materially* changes what that lens governs, not by whether a keyword appears. A decision that concentrates data or touches authority but creates no new external economics still runs the adversarial lens — as an **internal-integrity and reconciliation-abuse pass** — and may honestly conclude "lightly material, because …". Customer-outcomes runs on indirect effects too, but lightly, recording why. An honest "not material, because …" is a complete pass; a padded one is not. This reconciles §4 with the fraud-and-adversarial skill's own "no new economics — say so and stop" gate, which had been pulling the other way. The normative rule itself is left intact, so every prior record still conforms. See [TDR-0016](decisions/TDR-0016-routing-and-disposition-refinements-from-field-application.md).
- **Compound and thin records** (assurance-synthesis skill) — two shapes that recur in practice and are now handled explicitly rather than averaged. A record fusing two decisions of different risk is **split, not blended**. A thin or machine-derived record names its absent inputs as dated, owned decision-debt rather than inventing them, and any control the contradiction hunt must reason about but the record never proposed is **declared as inferred** — never averaged into a comfortable disposition.
- **Vacant-owner disposition** (DAC spec §5.2 item 11) — where the record under test names no accountable owner at all, the first constraint is to name one; a case that cannot name an owner for the decision itself cannot be `proceed-with-constraints`, and defers.
- **Internal-integrity variant** (fraud-and-adversarial skill) and the **indirect-effect pass** plus a **fair-lending signpost** (customer-outcomes skill): where a decision automates eligibility, pricing or credit, the cohort analysis *is* the indirect-discrimination test — thin-file, data-poor and protected-characteristic-correlated cohorts are where the foreseeable harm sits.
- **Trend versus trajectory** (DAC spec §6) — a cross-reference clarifying that the trend is a point-in-time direction, while non-monotonic shapes (risk that duplicates before it reduces, or grows only as adoption scales) are carried by `expected_trajectory` (§5.2.6), not compressed into the trend value.
- **Architecture overview brought level with v1.10.0** ([`ARCHITECTURE.md`](ARCHITECTURE.md)) — it had stood unchanged since v1.7.0 and predated the three-layer responsibility split and the projection concept. It now shows **projection** between the register and its consumers, and says per layer who enforces what: the record enforces nothing, the store controls access within its administrative domain, and a consumer assembles authorised projections. The boundary claim is unchanged; it is the enforcement picture that was missing.

- `spec.md` and `spec-decision-assurance.md` bumped to 1.11.0. Additive throughout: no field, template or schema change; every prior record still validates.

## [1.10.0] — 2026-07-24

### Added
- **Five recurring scope questions** (spec §4.4) — applicability, authority, **access**, **disclosure** and impact, offered as prompts rather than a complete ontology. They group into *operating scope* (applicability, authority, impact) and *information scope* (access, disclosure), with a third distinction — **use**, whether knowledge may influence an action — sitting outside the record entirely at runtime. **Access does not grant use, and use does not grant disclosure.** That someone may read a decision does not settle whether it may be repeated, to whom, or when. The disclosure condition is usually **a decision in its own right** — "communicate to affected customers only once colleague notification is complete" has an owner, an authority and a trigger — so the standard exists to record it, not to apply it. A workable condition must be prior to and independent of the act it gates. **Recording a disclosure decision is not an authorisation to disclose: capture never confers authority.**
- **Where the boundaries fall** (spec §3) — a companion to proportionality: create a separate record where a distinct accountable judgement can be **accepted, rejected, changed or superseded independently, without changing the remaining judgement**. Different owners, authority bases, rationales, applicability, outcomes or conditions are strong separation signals; separately available *evidence* is not, since almost any part of a decision has evidence of its own and splitting on that alone shatters one judgement into fragments nobody decided. **Execution events are evidence or triggers, not decisions, unless a further judgement was actually exercised.** Closing a site is five decisions (close · authorise preparation · approve the communication and its release conditions · approve redeployment · authorise complaints guidance); consultation completed and communication sent are events; whether the closure is effective is a computed state.
- **What this format does not conceal** (spec §10) — the standard now states its own scope and disclosure limits plainly. **This standard specifies capture**; who may see which records, how a view is authorised, and how any of it is enforced in use belong to the systems an adopter builds above the register. **A record does not enforce its own visibility**: a classification label is metadata for an enforcement layer to act on, not a control in itself. Identifier gaps, lineage references and downstream constraints disclose that a record exists and roughly what it concerned, so **redaction within a connected set is not concealment** — concealing existence requires separating the record, and separation is necessary but not sufficient, since caches, exports, logs and projections must respect the same boundary.
- **What this format does not yet compute** (spec §11) — `decision_date` says when a judgement was made, not when it took force; there is no effective-from, effective-until, suspended state or jurisdictional applicability. A body of TDRs therefore *supports* the current position without computing it. Time is also more than one date, and the moments are not all of the same kind: a change carries **decisions**, **events** that evidence or trigger them, and **states computed from both** — and confusing the three is how a register becomes untrustworthy, since an event recorded as a decision implies a judgement nobody made, and a computed state stored as a fact goes stale the moment its inputs change. An event-based condition is not a date: if the communication slips, the event has not happened. Also states the governed-vs-realised distinction: what was decided, implemented, controlled and actually happened are four different things.
- **A three-layer boundary contract** ([TDR-0015](decisions/TDR-0015-visibility-and-effectiveness.md)) — a table allocating each responsibility across the standard, the register or store, and the downstream platform, with deliberately narrow verbs: a store controls storage, existence visibility, retrieval, direct access and compartments *within its administrative domain*; a runtime governs *system-mediated* use, disclosure, aggregation and release, and *manages* inference risk rather than eliminating it. Stated plainly: **no layer can guarantee concealment, and none governs subsequent human use once information has been disclosed.** With two cautions that are easy to get wrong — **a record's `status` is never proof of operability, authority or permission to disclose**, and **capture confers nothing**. The standards-gap claim now carries its scope (a July 2026 review of the identity-and-delegation, access-control, usage-control, purpose-limitation, information-flow-control, declassification, inference-control and records-management literature) so that it can be challenged and superseded rather than merely asserted.
- **Adopter extension namespace** (spec §4.3, [`schema/tdr.schema.json`](records/decision/schema/tdr.schema.json)) — frontmatter fields prefixed `x-` now validate, so an organisation can carry its own classification, effective date or legal entity without forking the schema. The standard defines no vocabulary for them and no expectation of portability; anything broadly useful should be proposed for the core by superseding TDR.

### Changed
- `schema/tdr.schema.json` — the only schema change is a **relaxation**: `patternProperties` accepts `^x-`, while `additionalProperties: false` still holds for everything else, so a misspelled core field (`desicion_date`) continues to fail. Strictly more documents validate; no record that validated before stops.
- Practice guides gain the compartmentation limit and the assistant-leakage caution; the grammar notes that *Unless* is applicability, not visibility.
- `spec.md` bumped to 1.10.0. **No new field**; existing sections were not renumbered.

### Decided
- [TDR-0015](decisions/TDR-0015-visibility-and-effectiveness.md) — keeps visibility out of the record (a classification field would collide with every adopter's scheme, enforce nothing, and invite the belief that the label is the control), opens the `x-` namespace, and **defers** effective-from/until, a suspended state and jurisdictional applicability pending named adopter evidence. Recorded as a full record because the choice is hard to reverse: the reserved prefix and the register-not-record commitment both bind future releases.

## [1.9.0] — 2026-07-23

### Added
- **The Consequential Decision Grammar** ([`practice/decision-grammar.md`](records/decision/practice/decision-grammar.md)) — a candidate authoring grammar (v0.1) that gives a decision a speakable six-line shape: **Under · Given · We decide · This means · Unless · Until** (underneath: Authority · Basis · Choice · Effect · Boundary · Validity). It is a way of writing and thinking a decision that **compiles into the existing MTDR fields** — with the mapping table, clause guidance, anti-patterns, a consequentiality test, and fictional worked examples. Three points the guidance settles: *Under* covers statutory, policy and contractual authority, not only a delegated mandate; the actor in *We decide* is whoever holds the authority (an individual, a board, a forum), never a hidden "we"; and *Until* is validity, not duration — "until superseded" is complete, and inventing a review date to fill the field is an anti-pattern. The sharpest teaching point: *This means* is the **binding consequence**, not the hoped-for benefit — the benefit is a Value Record. Referenced from the capture and identification skills. See [TDR-0014](decisions/TDR-0014-consequential-decision-grammar.md).
